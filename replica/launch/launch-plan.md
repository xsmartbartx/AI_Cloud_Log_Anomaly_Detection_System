# Launch plan

Not started. This is the order, not a promise of dates.

## Before launch
1. Finish the blockers in replica/parity.md (Clerk, Console contract, Stripe test mode). No public launch with a must-have open.
2. Waitlist or beta: NEXORA's contact form is the only intake today (`/company#contact`). Add an email capture on the landing page once a processor is chosen (Clerk sign-up is enough for the free plan).
3. Analytics and error tracking: none installed. Pick privacy-friendly analytics and an error tracker, and list both as processors in the privacy policy (NEXORA's Terms and Privacy are still an unreviewed draft).
4. Trademark screening for "Linegist" (US, EU, WIPO) and a lawyer's view: not run yet. Do this before spending on assets.
5. Real logo (brief in replica/brand.md), OG image 1200x630, favicon set. Placeholder only today.
6. DNS: `linegist.onenexora.com` and a redirect from `sentinel.onenexora.com`; update the NEXORA product card and `/products/sentinel` copy and link.
7. Status page entry on status.onenexora.com.

## Where the audience is
From the research only Hacker News was read (28 comments). Reddit and review sites were not, so there is no evidence yet about which communities to post in. Read the relevant threads by hand first (log tooling cost, alert noise), then decide.

## Launch post
Show HN or Product Hunt, leading with the fix, not the feature list: "Paste a log, get the ten lines worth reading. Flat price per scan, hard cap, nothing stored." Be upfront that it is Beta, that analysis is rules plus statistics (no AI claim), and that it does not replace a log platform. Post only when the free plan works end to end with real sign-in. No fake upvotes, reviews or comments.

## First ten users
Talk to people by hand: existing NEXORA customers and contacts you already have, with their permission. Ask for one real log and watch them read the result. Record what they ignore; that feeds the mute feature. Use quotes only with written permission.

## Store listings
Linegist is a web product. The App Store and Google Play fields in `listing.json` (all pass the character limits and the avoid-list check) are ready only if a mobile wrapper is ever built. Apple's copycat rule is not an issue for the name, but a thin web wrapper is a different review risk.
